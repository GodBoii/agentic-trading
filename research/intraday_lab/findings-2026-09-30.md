# First research cycle

September 30, 2026. All results below are offline simulations or recording diagnostics. No live orders were sent and no production trading services were changed.

## Decision after this cycle

Keep the deterministic architecture and reject the current momentum baseline as a candidate for deployment. It has not established a trading advantage. Data provenance and executable-fill evidence need improvement before a faster strategy or a more complex model would be defensible.

The lab now provides a repeatable way to test that conclusion instead of relying on agent narratives. It also gives us a place to preserve losing hypotheses and investigate failures.

## What was built and checked

The package contains validated immutable observations, a causal policy, a fee model, an account-aware replay engine, a raw timestamp auditor, frozen experiment plans, input fingerprints, source snapshots, and trade journals. Its interfaces have no broker adapter or credential access.

Twenty-eight behavior tests pass. They cover causal decisions, long and short accounting, small-order costs, later-observation entry/exit, reservation limits, price drift, fill-time risk revalidation, loss halts, missing freshness, missing raw-capture scope, signed clock offsets, source-date selection, duplicate identities, and unresolved positions. Python compilation also passes.

An actual August 25 replay was repeated for all six configurations. Summaries matched after excluding runtime duration, trade CSVs were byte-identical, and normalized input hashes matched. The saved verification is [repeatability evidence](C:/Users/prajw/Downloads/Trader/research/intraday_lab/runs/baseline-v4-repeat-check/repeatability-verification.json).

This proves reproducibility on the inspected runtime and inputs. It does not prove exchange fills or portability across different numeric runtimes.

## Experimental design

The fixed universe contains twelve NSE cash instruments selected by descending historical ADV in the August 18 universe. That selection predates every replay session. Instruments were not chosen from the best later returns.

Replay dates are August 19, 20, 21, 24, 25, and 31, plus September 1. These recordings and some related historical reports have already been inspected. They are diagnostic sessions, not a pristine final holdout.

The selected tape contains 1,154,473 retained regular-session observations. They are downsampled received observations, not independent samples or full order events.

Six frozen policy/account comparisons ran across seven dates in two freshness modes, yielding 84 final policy/session replays. The standard illustrative paper account begins each date at Rs 500,000, with position value capped at Rs 100,000, three committed slots, Rs 500 planned risk per trade, and a Rs 2,500 daily loss-action threshold. The smaller account scenario uses Rs 100,000 equity and lower monetary limits.

Signals require a 300-second rolling history, a directional 60-second confirmation, and controlled spread. Base target and stop thresholds are 30 and 15 bps. These were specified before reading the outcomes and were not optimized afterward.

The fill model uses the next usable same-instrument observation after simulated arrival. Longs enter at ask and exit at bid; shorts reverse those sides. Hypothetical extra slippage and rounded per-order fees apply. Displayed top-five aggregate depth limits size but does not prove best-quote capacity. No passive queues or partial fills are modeled.

## Receipt-proxy experiment

This separate mode permits unknown flags and does not require a recent last trade. It assumes quote usability from received observations and continuity. It keeps the source freshness uncertainty explicit and is never eligible for promotion.

Its purpose is to exercise strategy economics and execution/risk behavior on more of the recordings. These numbers do not establish tradable live performance.

| Comparison | Simulated trades | Gross P&L, Rs | Fees, Rs | Net P&L, Rs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Momentum baseline | 155 | -5,944.02 | 11,698.51 | -17,642.53 |
| VWAP alignment | 153 | -6,234.01 | 11,520.65 | -17,754.66 |
| Cost-room gate | 153 | -6,234.01 | 11,520.65 | -17,754.66 |
| Cost-room, 1,000 ms arrival delay | 153 | -6,211.51 | 11,520.66 | -17,732.17 |
| Cost-room, 3 bps extra slippage per side | 117 | -9,112.19 | 8,757.75 | -17,869.94 |
| Cost-room, smaller paper account | 119 | -1,042.99 | 2,506.80 | -3,549.79 |

These are sums of separate daily scenarios. The smaller account has different exposure and loss-action limits, so its lower monetary loss is not a strategy improvement.

The baseline is losing before fees. Fees account for roughly two-thirds of its total net loss. Gross profit occurs in 46 trades and net profit in only 26. Its exits comprise 78 time exits, 56 stops, eleven daily-loss exits, and ten targets.

VWAP alignment does not improve the result in this experiment. The cost-room gate does not reject additional admitted trades under these thresholds. Sufficient room to a hypothetical target is not proof that price will reach it.

The delay comparison changes little here. Baseline entry waits have a median of about 1.37 seconds and p95 of about 3.07 seconds because execution waits for the next retained observation. This coarse tape cannot substantiate a fine-grained comparison between 250 ms and 1,000 ms true exchange execution. Those waits are simulator observation intervals, not actual broker fill latency.

All final simulations end without unresolved positions or pending entries. Tests separately establish that incomplete data produces unresolved exposure rather than a fabricated exit. Maximum marked intraday drawdown in the proxy baseline reaches about Rs 2,860 despite the Rs 2,500 action threshold. The threshold initiates risk actions; delayed exits can exceed it.

## Improvements made during the cycle

1. The first smoke run read ADV from the wrong location in the universe schema. The selector now uses `historical.adv_20_cr`, rejects unavailable ADV, and has a regression test. The old run is preserved as invalid selection evidence.
2. Missing freshness flags originally collapsed to false values. The contracts now preserve unknown values, and diagnostics distinguish missing evidence from an explicit bad state. Strict behavior stays conservative.
3. Receipt-based eligibility is an explicit separate mode rather than an unnoticed change to the strategy's freshness assumptions.
4. Planned exposure and loss limits are rechecked at the actual simulated entry price, preventing admission-time sizing from silently exceeding the position cap after a price change.
5. Raw capture schema drift is handled explicitly. Missing capture scope becomes unknown, while missing required packet identity fails the audit.
6. New runs preserve runtime versions and a copy of the engine source in addition to fingerprints.

None of these corrections was designed to maximize historical returns. They repair the meaning and reliability of the experiment.

## Next build

The next useful implementation is a recorder/normalizer contract that separates trade timestamps, quote timestamps where supplied, local receipt, processing delay, sequence/reconnect evidence, and reference-data versions. Preserve every packet for a cohort selected in advance. A reconnect or full consumer queue must create an explicit quality event.

Then build a raw-full-packet replay adapter with real top-level quantity and explicit provenance. Warmup inferred from receipt continuity must remain labeled as inferred; it is not a stored network connection state. Negative timestamp offsets must remain visible. The existing sparse hot-only archives will still need coverage reporting.

Before broker integration, add daily trading-status/corporate-action checks, verified tick-size conventions, partial-fill handling, and durable unknown-submission recovery. Before strategy promotion, require fresh independently evaluated data and calibrated fill/cost behavior. Do not train a neural network on these coarse outcome labels and treat prediction accuracy as executable edge.

The current result is a tested research engine and a rejected first hypothesis. That is a useful starting point for the next iteration.
