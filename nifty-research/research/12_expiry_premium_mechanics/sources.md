# Primary sources reviewed on 2 October 2026

- [Stoll, The relationship between put and call option prices, 1969](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1969.tb01694.x). Original parity paper and publication record. This study inherits study03's same-expiry parity forward and its common bid/ask interval filter. It does not replicate Stoll's original empirical sample.
- [Black, The pricing of commodity contracts, 1976](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304405X76900246). Original publication record and abstract for forward/futures option pricing. The pricing and inversion functions reused here come from study03's local implementation. A same-expiry forward is inferred from option parity. The monthly Nifty future is used only to describe observed movement, not substituted into weekly IV.
- [Gatheral and Jacquier, Arbitrage-free SVI volatility surfaces](https://arxiv.org/html/1204.0646v4). Section 1 explains call prices decreasing and convex in strike under static consistency. This study tests discrete payoff bounds and quote intervals. It neither fits SVI nor reproduces their SPX calibration.
- [QuantLib Black formula implementation](https://github.com/lballabio/QuantLib/blob/master/ql/pricingengines/blackformula.cpp). Primary pricing implementation reviewed as an independent reference for discounted Black values. No QuantLib installation or whole-repository backtest ran.
- [vollib reference Black source, published documentation](https://vollib.org/documentation/1.0.3/_modules/py_vollib/ref_python/black.html). The original GitHub path linked by study03 did not resolve in this browsing session; its published source documentation did. Numerical flat-volatility tests here use study03's local formula. No trading code was imported from an external repository.

Vertical bounds also follow directly from terminal payoffs. For K1<K2, a call vertical C1-C2 and put vertical P2-P1 both pay between 0 and K2-K1. Discounted values therefore lie between 0 and D times the strike width under the declared valuation assumptions. The unequal-strike butterfly uses positive endpoint weights summing to one and a short middle option. Its terminal payoff is nonnegative. The tests verify these payoff identities directly on synthetic underlying grids.

Rates of 0% and 10% are research sensitivities. They are not historical Indian financing quotes. Quote-side checks omit taxes, financing, margin, size and execution. Primary sources establish theoretical relationships, not executable opportunities in this retail recording.
